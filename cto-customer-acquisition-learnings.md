## Part 1: What We've Learned About Finding Customers

### The Jyoti Bansal Framework in Practice

Over 6 months of customer pursuit, three principles proved transformative:

#### 1. **"Extreme Pain" Principle - Validated**

**Framework:** Find customers whose pain is so severe they'll work with a startup at 80% product completeness.

**What I learned:**
- "Interesting problem" ≠ "extreme pain"
- Extreme pain has **quantified cost** (lost revenue, blocked deals, churn risk)
- Extreme pain has **urgency triggers** (deadline, crisis, competitive threat)
- Extreme pain creates **openness to startups** (desperate buyers take risks)

**Real Examples:**

| Target | Pain Level | Result |
|--------|------------|--------|

| **Anomalix** | Need to break into NERM space. Zero market presence. Technical product needs expert positioning. | ✅ **Strong lead** |
| **Bulla/BDC** | AI education needed, but no crisis | ⚠️ **Nurturing** (slow) |
| **Stock Analysis GPT** | Nice-to-have demo | ⚠️ **Nurturing** (slow) |

**Key Insight:** Pain severity directly correlates to sales velocity. Pextra couldn't afford to wait (product ready, market opportunity active, can't hire). Bulla/BDC can wait (education, not crisis).

---

#### 2. **The "3 Whys" - Essential Discovery Questions**

**Framework Questions:**
1. Why buy anything? (What's the cost of doing nothing?)
2. Why buy now? (What's the urgency trigger?)
3. Why buy us? (What's our unique fit?)

**How I Applied This:**

#### 3. **80/20 Product Rule - The "Good Enough" Principle**

**Framework:** At 80% product completeness, deliver immediate value for one painful use case.

**What I Discovered:**
We've been using Claude to simulate what Mobsta will do. The assets I've created are **~80% complete** but deliver **immediate value**:

- Pextra got comprehensive GTM partnership playbook (12 specific deliverables, 12-week timeline)
- Anomalix got market analysis + positioning framework + competitive landscape
- Auth0 demo got technical implementation guide + code examples

**Critical Realization:** The 80% deliverables using Claude ARE the product demonstration. They prove we can deliver expertise at speed.

**Objection Handling That Worked:**

| Objection | Counter That Closed |
|-----------|-------------------|
| "You're not a big vendor" | "Exactly—we're specialized, responsive, fast-moving. Big vendors take 6 months to deliver what we do in 2 weeks." |
| "Your product isn't finished" | "True—and that's why it adapts to YOUR environment right now. We co-build with you, not force you into our box." |
| "We'll revisit later" | "This problem costs you $X every month you wait. Solving it now prevents [quantified impact]." |

## Part 2: The Asset Creation Process (What Works)

### Current Process Using Claude

Over 9 playbooks, I've refined a repeatable 5-step process:

#### Step 1: Deep Research (1-2 hours)
**Inputs gathered:**
- LinkedIn profile of key contact (PDF export)
- Company website analysis
- Industry research (pain points, competitive landscape)
- Direct pain statements from conversations
- Jyoti framework analysis (extreme pain identification)



**Example (Pextra):**
- Input: LinkedIn profile, company website, "we need SE help" statement
- Analysis revealed: Product ready, can't hire fast enough, Broadcom crisis = market opportunity
- Pain quantified: $500K+ pipeline stuck waiting for SE capacity every quarter
- Urgency: Can't wait 6 months, competitors moving now

---


1. **Executive Summary** (prove we get it immediately)
2. **Pain Point Analysis** (show depth of understanding)
3. **Quantified Impact** (numbers, not theory)
4. **Alternative Solutions Analysis** (why they haven't solved it yet)
5. **Proposed Approach** (our unique solution)
6. **Success Metrics** (how we'll measure value)

**Multiple Perspectives Analysis:**
While creating the pain report, I analyze from multiple angles:
- Business analyst perspective: "Here's the revenue impact"
- Technical expert perspective: "Here's how to solve it technically"
- Competitive analyst perspective: "Here's why alternatives fail"
- Customer perspective: "Here's what matters to the buyer"

**Note on Mobsta positioning:** In playbooks like Pextra, I position multi-agent capabilities as a roadmap item that could integrate directly into their platform—they loved this vision of collaboration at scale.

**Critical:** This report demonstrates expertise BEFORE any sales conversation. It answers objections preemptively.

---

#### Step 3: Tailored Deliverable Creation (2-4 hours)
**Different formats for different needs:**

**A. Education Track (AI novices):**
- **Output:** "AI Playbook for [Company/Use Case]"
- **Content:** Prompt libraries, use case examples, tool comparisons
- **Value:** Immediate ROI with existing tools (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.)
- **Example:** Bulla Legal AI Playbook, BDC AI Playbook

**B. Advanced Education Track (AI-savvy buyers):**
- **Output:** "Advanced AI Strategies for [Domain]"
- **Content:** Multi-agent workflows, automation frameworks, integration strategies
- **Value:** Maximize existing tool investments
- **Example:** Stock Analysis AI Playbook

**C. Technical Partnership Track (SE/GTM needs):**
- **Output:** "GTM Partnership Playbook" or "Market Entry Strategy"
- **Content:** Target customer lists, positioning frameworks, technical deliverables, timeline
- **Value:** Expert capacity at startup-friendly pricing
- **Example:** Pextra Partnership Playbook, Anomalix GTM Playbook

**D. ICP Refinement Track (sales struggling with qualification):**
- **Output:** "Customer Urgency Analysis" or "Pain Point Mapping"
- **Content:** ICP characteristics, pain qualification frameworks, urgency signals
- **Value:** Stop chasing nice-to-have deals, find crisis-driven buyers
- **Example:** Yardi Problems Analysis

---

#### Step 4: Interactive Format (1-2 hours)
**Why HTML playbooks work:**
- Professional, polished presentation
- Interactive elements (expandable sections, navigation)
- Self-contained (no platform required)
- Shareable within organization
- Demonstrates technical capability

**Structure I've been using:**
```html
- Executive Summary (1-2 paragraph TL;DR)
- Pain Analysis (quantified)
- Solution Framework (specific, actionable)
- Implementation Timeline (concrete milestones)
- Success Metrics (measurable outcomes)
- Next Steps (clear call-to-action)
```

**Tools:** Claude Code for HTML generation, refined through iterations.

### The Nurture Strategy: Education → Trust → Partnership

**Pattern recognition:**
Nurture assets serve a different purpose than close assets:

| Close Assets | Nurture Assets |
|--------------|----------------|
| Extreme pain focus | Educational value |
| Urgent timeline | Long-term relationship |
| Specific deliverables | Strategic guidance |
| Fast decision makers | Complex organizations |

**Both are valuable, but for different stages:**
- **Nurture assets** build pipeline and credibility
- **Close assets** convert when urgency emerges

**The mistake:** Expecting nurture assets to close immediately. They won't. That's fine—they're doing their job.

## What Automation Might Address (Based on Manual Process Pain Points)

**Current manual process observed:**
- Requires 15-30 Claude prompts per playbook
- Manually simulate multiple expert perspectives
- Stitch together outputs across multiple chat sessions
- Takes 4-8 hours per comprehensive playbook
- 50-100% time overhead on orchestration (context management, consistency checking)

**Patterns that suggest potential automation value:**
- Research phase could be systematized (company URL, LinkedIn, pain signals → structured analysis)
- Jyoti "3 Whys" framework could be consistently applied (currently manual interpretation)
- Multi-perspective generation could be orchestrated (currently sequential manual prompting)
- 80% complete deliverable format could be templated (currently recreated each time)

**Observation on potential speedup:**
If orchestration overhead (50-100%) could be eliminated and multi-perspective generation could be parallelized, the manual process time (4-8 hours) might reduce significantly. Field experience suggests the actual creation time (not orchestration) is ~2-3 hours, which could potentially compress further with automation.

**Intent to explore:** Whether automation should prioritize workflow orchestration (eliminating overhead) over AI quality improvements (which already appears sufficient based on customer engagement).

**Observation:** Proposal structure is repeatable across clients, but currently recreated manually each time.

**Potential value if automated:** If proposal generation could follow proven template with client-specific customization, could reduce from 1-2 hours to 15-20 minutes.

## Observation on Time Investment and Potential Efficiency Gains

- Research phase: 1-2 hours
- Analysis phase: 2-3 hours

- Created 9 playbooks manually in 6 months (~50 hours total human time)
