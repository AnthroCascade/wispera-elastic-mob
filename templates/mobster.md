## Mobster Specification Components

Beyond the skill-block composition, a mobster would need:

### Identity & Purpose
**Role Name**: What this mobster represents (e.g., "Full-Stack Developer", "Security Architect", "Product Owner")  
**Core Mission**: The overarching purpose this mobster serves in the development conversation  
**Perspective Lens**: The unique viewpoint this mobster brings to discussions

### Skill-Block Composition
**Primary Skills**: Core skill-blocks that define this mobster's expertise  
**Secondary Skills**: Supporting skill-blocks for broader context  
**Skill Adaptations**: Specific overrides, reinforcements, or biases applied to skill-blocks  
(e.g., "Database skills with strong preference for ACID compliance")

### Game Configuration
**Primary Game**: The main optimization game this mobster plays  
**Game Weights**: How strongly different success metrics are weighted  
**Conflict Resolution Style**: How this mobster handles competing priorities  
**Negotiation Patterns**: Typical compromise strategies

### Mob Participation Dynamics
**Engagement Threshold**: How relevant a conversation must be to activate participation  
**Preferred Mob Roles**: Natural affinity for Driver/Navigator/Facilitator/Scout/Housekeeper
**Energy Patterns**: When this mobster tends to lead vs. support  
**Collaboration Preferences**: Which other mobsters this one works best/worst with

### Communication Characteristics
**Discourse Style**: How this mobster typically communicates (analytical, creative, pragmatic)  
**Assertion Confidence**: How strongly this mobster advocates for its positions  
**Question Patterns**: Types of clarifying questions this mobster tends to ask  
**Vocabulary Tendencies**: Domain-specific language or jargon usage

### Meta-Behaviors
**Learning Patterns**: How this mobster adapts based on project outcomes  
**Pattern Recognition**: What types of problems this mobster is quickest to identify  
**Escalation Triggers**: When this mobster calls for broader mob participation

The key is that a mobster is more than just a bundle of skills - it's a coherent perspective with its own goals, communication style, and participation dynamics. It's the difference between having database knowledge and being a Database Architect who champions data integrity in every conversation.
